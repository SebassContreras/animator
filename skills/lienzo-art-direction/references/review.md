# Review and references

## Frame critique

Run on each `scene_preview` the animate workflow already takes. Every "no" becomes a fix with layer, property and value.

1. **Focal point.** Can you tell in 1 s what to look at? Only one element has the accent colour and the highest contrast.
2. **Silhouettes.** Does each object read without its details? Squint at the image.
3. **Hierarchy.** Big / medium / small sizes present; text smaller than the image it supports.
4. **Depth.** Background, middle and foreground planes visible; far layers lighter.
5. **Light.** One light direction, the same on every object.
6. **Consistency.** One style: same radius, outline width, shading and type across shots.
7. **Negative space.** Margins kept (`craft.md` → Framing); nothing crowds the edges or the text.
8. **Contrast.** Text ≥ 4.5:1 against what is behind it.
9. **Motion** (check before and after the frame). Nothing linear except travel; nothing frozen; entries staggered.
10. **Designed, not generated.** Nothing looks like a default: no unwanted gradients or shadows (see gotchas), no perfectly even rows of identical copies.

## Where to look

Give the user 3–4 links per piece and say what to take from each (palette, shapes, rhythm, transitions).

| Source | Take from it |
|---|---|
| Kurzgesagt, TED-Ed (YouTube) | flat vector explainers: rounded shapes, flat shading, glow, depth |

| Disney's 12 principles; *The Animator's Survival Kit* (R. Williams) | the theory behind `motion-polish.md` |

Note on naming: in Disney's terms "ease out" means slow start, the opposite of CSS-style easing names. Lienzo uses CSS-style names (`cubic-out` = decelerates at the end).
